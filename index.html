Where it worked, and where it didn't

WorkedNever used "all records". Strong column and entity selection. Creation mode averaged 76.3% compliance across 24 cases; optimization mode 93.2%.
Didn'tOwnership filters failed in 16 of 24 creation cases. Role and category filters were missing in half. Profiles with 100K+ records strained sync.

This is the next product decision: better role-based filters came first on my roadmap, then a natural-language interface, Maker Portal integration, pagination for large data, and a verifier that runs the app and checks sync automatically.

Then on a real client's data

The client is not named. Their offline profile was 5.2 GB against a recommended 4 GB or less, causing sync failures and a poor offline experience. This was a test on their original data, not a release.

What changed: time-bounded filters on appointments and order products, custom entities scoped to active data, historical data removed, reference data trimmed to current versions, sync intervals balanced for freshness against performance.

Case study 2 · Independent product analysis · Nov 2026

Google Pay: a help assistant that explains, then routes

Independent analysis, not affiliated with or endorsed by Google. Built only from public information and my own small tests.

RoleProduct owner, end to end
Method2,000 reviews, 22-question test, 4 runs
OutputA 3.5-page PRD with metrics and rollout
MarketIndia, UPI-first

The user problem

A user sees a fee they didn't expect, a reward that never came, a failed payment or a "payments blocked" message. They can't tell why it happened or whom to contact. The Help Center search guesses a topic and lists articles; the human path exists, but I found it only through a Google search.

Result

~7,000contacts avoided per 100,000 sessions (my assumption)

The product: explain, then route

Every question is checked against approved sources, then sent to one of five lanes. The test failures above became the design rules.

Check the approved sources · Tier 1: Google Pay help pages · Tier 2: RBI, NPCI and cybercrime portal pages
1 · ResolveA rule answers it. Short, cited, one next step.
2 · Not yetA time window is still open. Say when to check.
3 · RouteNeeds account data. Name the right party, pre-fill the form.
4 · UrgentMoney at risk now. Bank, 1930 and the fraud line first.
5 · RefuseUnsafe request. Decline, explain, offer a safe path.
On every lane: source and date shown · a human option always visible · never accepts or repeats a PIN or OTP

What it looks like

A fee question, lane 1.

Why was I charged on my recharge?
Two fees can apply to a recharge. Platform fee: applies to UPI recharges too. Convenience fee: card payments only. The exact amount shows on the payment screen and in your transaction history.Source: Google Pay Help, "Recharge your prepaid mobile phone"
Yes, this helpedNo, talk to support

Edge cases I designed for

PIN or OTP typed into chatNever repeat it, tell the user to change it, don't keep the text.
"Ignore your rules" and bypass requestsRefuse, then offer the legitimate path.
Rules changeShow the source date. The biometric limit changed in Aug 2026.
Vague inputAsk one short question first.

How I'd measure it

GoalA user with a money problem gets the right answer, or the right human, on the first try.
Primary (north star) metricShare of assistant sessions with no repeat contact on the same issue within 7 days. It rises only if the problem was solved or correctly handed off, and it can't be gamed by hiding the human option.
SupportingCorrect-lane rate, share of answers naming a source, handoff completion, "Did this help?" rate.
GuardrailsWrong money facts per 1,000 answers (stay near zero), any PIN or OTP acceptance, time to a human in the urgent lane, complaint rate, cost per resolved session.

An A/B test against today's search experience, then staged exposure. Any wrong amount, any accepted PIN or OTP, or a complaint spike rolls the lane back.

A root-cause agent, and a platform my team could use to stress test it

Two products for two users: customers whose Redis incidents the agent investigates, and my own team, who needed realistic failures to stress test their products.

RoleOwned both, solo

Product 1, for customers: the Redis root-cause agent

20–30 minto investigate an incident by hand
→
Secondswith the agent, at 96.4% accuracy

The risk with an agent that touches live systems is a confident wrong action. So I designed the failure path first.

IncidentAn alert, or a cache miss that raises no alert.
InvestigateThe agent plans and runs the checks. Built as 7 services.
ValidateA two-tier, read-only validator checks every step.
Fall back safelyTiered fallbacks when data is missing or a check fails.

I mapped 5 behavior patterns and 9 failure modes, including detecting a cache-miss problem with no alert at all.
